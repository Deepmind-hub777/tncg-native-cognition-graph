# Contributing to TNCG

Contributions are welcome in research, language examples, annotation methods, pedagogy, technical design, and evaluation.

## Standards

1. Cite sources for factual, linguistic, historical, and cultural claims.
2. Label statements as evidence, interpretation, or hypothesis.
3. Preserve semantic distinctions; do not force false equivalence across languages.
4. Include register, region, context, and example usage where relevant.
5. Avoid cultural essentialism: no single example represents an entire culture.
6. Submit only material you created or are authorized to share.
7. Never submit identifiable student records, private conversations, or sensitive personal data.

## Suggested concept record

- Concept ID
- Language and native form
- Source-language definition
- Contextual examples
- Related and contrasting concepts
- Possible translations and their limits
- Cultural or pragmatic context, with evidence
- Sources, confidence, and open questions

Keep pull requests focused. Explain the problem, proposed change, sources, and limitations. For major changes, open an issue first. Respectful disagreement is welcome; document unresolved differences rather than concealing them.
